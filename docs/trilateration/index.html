Trilateration exercise: three control points, three measured points

Slate data from the dive team (divers Rani, Emily and Jaen, names as read from the slate). Each diver measured one range from each of control points A, B and C to a point on the object: high, mid and low. Frame: A is the origin, +X runs toward B, +Y toward the C side, +Z is up from a flat seabed. All distances are in metres.

Read this first: the slate gives B–C and C–A without a metres digit (“40 cm”, “44 cm”) and also shows “3.4 M” beside B–C. I used B–C = 2.40 m and C–A = 2.44 m. The ranges rule out 3.40 m (see Table 5). Even so, Emily’s mid point cannot be placed exactly: her A and C ranges fall 34 cm short of spanning A–C, so it is drawn as a low-confidence best fit (Tables 3 and 4).

Why the mid point fails: A–mid plus C–mid is 2.10 m, but A–C is 2.44 m, so even a point sitting on the line A–C could not satisfy both ranges. They are 34 cm short in total. The best fit sits on the seabed (Z = 0) and needs both ranges about 17 cm longer. A mis-read or mis-recorded tape value is the likely cause. Treat the mid point as uncertain by about ±0.2 m, and ask Emily to recheck her A and C readings.

Method and assumptions

  • Datum: A = (0, 0, 0), B = (AB, 0, 0), C on the +Y side. Control points are level and on a flat seabed (Z = 0). The mirror image (C on −Y) is equally valid, since nothing on the slate fixes handedness.
  • Trilateration: with distances dA, dB, dC to a point, x = (dA² − dB² + AB²) / 2AB, y = (dA² − dC² + Cx² + Cy² − 2·x·Cx) / 2Cy, z = +√(dA² − x² − y²). The positive root is taken because every measured point is above the control points.
  • When ranges do not close (z² < 0, or the spheres miss each other): a bounded least-squares fit with the control triangle held fixed and Z ≥ 0. That is the case for the mid point.
  • Readings: “1m 7cm” is 1.07 m, “2.6 M” is 2.60 m, and “1M 30CM” (written under the table) confirms C–low = 1.30 m. Ranges are taken as straight-line tape distances.
  • Not modelled: tape sag, slack, offsets from the tape-end to the object surface, and which part of the object each diver actually touched. The three measured points are independent; the object is not assumed to be vertical.
